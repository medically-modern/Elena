MANAGER — MEDICAL EVALUATION / MEDICAL NECESSITY SOP (Manager Dashboard). Role: Manager (currently Janelle). Tool: Pipeline Oversight Dashboard (Command Center). Scope: the entire Medical Necessity queue.

YOUR JOB IN ONE LINE - Work the entire Medical Necessity queue from the Pipeline Oversight Dashboard. When a case escalates to you — a processor has exhausted their 3 attempts — it's yours to take over: diagnose what went wrong from their notes, fix it, advance the patient, and coach the processor so it doesn't happen again. For everything that hasn't escalated, your job is to watch: peek into buckets, read notes, catch mistakes early, and coach before a case ever reaches you.

THE TWO HALVES OF THE JOB:
- Reactive — own what's escalated. Any case where a processor has exhausted their 3 attempts (Confirm Receipt or either Chase Clinicals bucket) comes to you. You take full ownership of it — this is NOT a hand-back to the processor.
- Proactive — watch everything else. The rest of the queue isn't escalated, but it's still yours to monitor. Peek into buckets, read the notes processors are leaving, and coach on what you see before it turns into a third exhausted attempt.
Both halves are worked from the dashboard — it's where you watch the queue and where you'll usually start when a case lands on you.

WORKING AN ESCALATED CASE (end to end):
- Diagnose. Read the processor's notes from every attempt — what they sent, who they contacted, what came back. Figure out exactly why it stalled: wrong template, wrong contact, missed cadence, a chart gap nobody caught, or something outside the processor's control.
- Fix it. Take whatever action actually resolves the case — a different outreach channel, a different contact at the clinic, or going back further in the chain to correct a bad Evaluate decision. You're not just attempting again the same way that already failed three times.
- Advance the patient. Get the case moving again — confirmed receipt, records in hand, or back through Evaluate for a fresh read — so it doesn't sit stalled behind the escalation.
- Coach the processor. This step isn't optional. Go back to whoever owned the case and walk through what happened and what to do differently. The point of taking the case isn't just to close it — it's to make sure the same processor doesn't escalate the same way again.

WATCHING THE LIVE BUCKETS (scanning, not processing):
- Peek into Evaluate, Send Request, Confirm Receipt, and Chase Clinicals — across all processors, not just the ones who've had problems before.
- Read notes as they're logged. The notes are where you catch a mistake forming — a vague Send Request note, a Confirm Receipt attempt logged out of cadence, language marked "Yes" that looks thin.
- Anticipate, don't just react. If you can see a case heading toward Attempt #3 before it gets there, that's a coaching moment now, cheaper than a takeover later.
- Coach in the moment. A quick note or a quick conversation when you spot something is the mechanism that's supposed to shrink the escalation pile over time.

THE TRAJECTORY YOU'RE MANAGING TOWARD - Expect a lot of cases to escalate to you early on — that's normal while processors are still learning the stage. Your proactive coaching is what's supposed to bend that curve down: as processors internalize what you're catching and correcting, fewer cases should exhaust 3 attempts and land on you. A shrinking escalation volume over time is the signal the coaching is working — not a sign there's less for you to do.
