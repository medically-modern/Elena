MEDICAL NECESSITY — ROLES & STAGE-FLOW OVERVIEW (SOP index). Stage: Medical Necessity (Medical Evaluation board). This index ties the Medical Necessity sub-stage SOPs together and maps each sub-stage to its owner. Stage-flow and roles below are transcribed from the training document's diagram pages.

STAGE FLOW:
- Stage 1 — Evaluate MN: the decision engine. Every fresh review — first pass or a loop-back — happens here.
- Stage 2 — Send Request: turn the gap into a request, sent via Fax, Parachute, or Email.
- Stage 3 (Fax lane) — Confirm Receipt: confirm the fax was received. Up to 3 attempts, 1 business day apart.
- Stage 4 (Fax lane) — Chase Fax Clinicals: pursue the records via fax. Up to 3 attempts, +3 business days each.
- Stage 4 (Parachute / Email lane) — Chase Parachute / Email Clinicals: the same pursuit on the Parachute/Email track (this lane skips Confirm Receipt). Up to 3 attempts, +3 business days each.
- New Clinicals: new faxes / Parachute messages / emails are reviewed at Evaluate MN. New clinicals route back to Evaluate MN for a fresh pass whenever they arrive — the Evaluate Attempt counter increments by one each time.

ROLES & RESPONSIBILITIES (by owner and sub-stages owned):
- Processor — Masheke: owns Evaluate MN, Send Request, Chase Parachute / Email Clinicals, and Review New Clinicals. Focus: internal documentation processing — meticulous detail and ensuring the chart language is comprehensive.
- Processor — Maddie: owns Confirm Receipt and Chase Fax Clinicals. Focus: outbound calls and navigating doctor offices to wrangle documents.
- Manager — Janelle: oversees Medical Necessity and escalated cases. Owns a case once Confirm Receipt or any Chase bucket exceeds attempt 3, or once a patient is sent back to Evaluate MN for attempt 3. Does the task directly for the patient, then dissects what happened and trains the rep so these cases happen less over time. Oversees processors daily, ensuring tasks are completed timely and comprehensively.
(Note: the source document labels these "Role 1" and "Role 2" inconsistently between its diagram page and its index page; the owner-to-sub-stage mapping above is consistent across both and is the reliable reference.)

COORDINATION & COMMUNICATION - Clear communication keeps cases moving — with the doctor's office, and between Role 1 and Role 2 — and is what keeps cases out of Manager Review.
- Why round 3 happens, and how we prevent it: two failed rounds with the doctor's office is a signal that something is getting lost in translation. They don't know what's needed to get a patient covered — our job is hand-feeding them the exact, basic sentences to document. (a) Spell it out on the digital request in plain language, not just a template name. (b) Spell it out again on the call — restate it verbally to whoever picks up; don't assume the written request alone lands.
- For Role 1, Role 2, and Manager Review to work fluidly, comprehensive notes and information have to pass between them at every handoff: what Role 2 found missing is what Role 1 is chasing; what Role 1 gets back is what Role 2 re-evaluates; and what reaches round 3 is exactly what the Manager needs to dissect.
