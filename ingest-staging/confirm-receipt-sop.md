CONFIRM RECEIPT OF FAX — PROCESSOR / REP SOP. Stage: Medical Necessity → Confirm Receipt (Fax lane). Owner role: Processor (currently Maddie). Goal: get a real person at the clinic to confirm they have our request — and put a name on it, every time.

YOUR JOB IN ONE LINE - Get a real person at the clinic to confirm they have our request, and get their name and title attached to that confirmation.

CADENCE - Up to 3 attempts, 1 business day apart. You're chasing an acknowledgment, not the records themselves — but you don't hang up without a name.

WHY A NAME MATTERS - Assume this is a sleepy little doctor's office — small staff, busy front desk, faxes that sit in a tray for days. Nobody there is going to chase this for us; that's exactly why this step exists: not to get the records, just to get a person on record saying "yes, we have it." If we skip this and go straight to Chase Clinicals, the clinic can say "we never got that." If we've already confirmed with a named person (e.g., Diane at front desk, Tuesday at 2:14pm), we can say "no — Diane already confirmed this on the 14th." A name and title is the difference between those two conversations.

WORK THE CALL, STEP BY STEP:
- Send a courtesy fax first. Before you call, send a courtesy fax so the request is already sitting in their inbox when you reach them.
- Get a person on the phone. Call and work the dial pad until you reach an actual human who can check a fax tray — not a voicemail box.
- Read the room and ask. Be friendly, be quick. Ask whether they received the fax, or whether they can take a second to check.
- If they don't see it in the patient's file, point them to it. "Great — I actually sent a courtesy fax right before this call, so it should be right at the top of your inbox."
- If they still don't have it, get it into their hands any way you can. Email it, resend the fax, ask if they're set up on Parachute. Don't hang up until something has landed.
- Log the confirmation. Mark receipt confirmed and record the individual's name, their role, and any detail worth carrying forward — turnaround time, who else is involved. e.g. "Spoke with Diane, front desk. She confirmed receipt and said they'll get it back to us by end of day."
- Log the attempt either way. If you didn't get a confirmation, document specifically why — not just "unsuccessful." e.g. "On hold 12 minutes, then hung up on." "Reached someone who said it's the wrong clinic." "They said their fax line is down."

KNOW WHAT YOU'RE ACTUALLY CONFIRMING (check the MN Workflow Note before you dial):
- New request (1st time) — this is about getting documents. They haven't sent us anything yet. Walk them through exactly what we need, plainly: "we just need the chart notes and the CGM script for [patient]." Keep it simple — this is a delivery problem, not an explanation problem.
- Insufficient documentation (2nd+ time) — this needs hand-holding. They already sent something, but the coverage language we need isn't in it. The front desk and even the provider often don't know payer coverage rules — they don't know what "medical necessity language" means or why their note isn't enough. Don't lead with the rule — lead with the fix. Acknowledge what they sent ("we did get the chart notes, thank you"), then translate the gap into one plain, concrete sentence they can act on — something the provider can literally add to the note. That "can you just add this one little sentence so the patient can be covered" move turns an abstract compliance requirement into a two-minute easy action.

TRICKS OF THE TRADE (work it like a sales call — persistence, not paperwork):
- Send the courtesy fax before you call — you want to be asking about something that's already there.
- Ask for one small, specific thing, never a vague pile of records. "Can you just add one sentence to the note?" is an easy yes; "Can you re-document medical necessity?" is not.
- Use their own language back to them — repeat the patient's name, the doctor's name — anything that signals this is routine and quick.
- If the first person can't help, ask who can; don't accept a dead end as the answer.
- Offer every channel you have — fax, email, Parachute, fax again. Remove every excuse to put it off.
- Always get a name and title before you hang up, even on a "yes, we'll take care of it." A promise with no name attached isn't a confirmation.

WHAT HAPPENS NEXT:
- Receipt confirmed: the case advances to Chase Clinicals, where we pursue the actual records (up to 3 attempts, 3 business days apart). Your note — name, title, what they said, expected turnaround — travels with the case.
- Not confirmed: log the attempt with specifics (who you reached, what they said, what's still outstanding), wait one business day, then try again — up to 3 attempts total before escalation.
- Exhaustion path: after 3 attempts, if you are unsuccessful in confirming receipt, this gets escalated to your manager, who will execute it and then train you on what could have been done differently next time.
